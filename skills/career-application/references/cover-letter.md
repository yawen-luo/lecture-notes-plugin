# Cover Letter — drafting, tailoring, and recruiter-oriented quality control

Load this reference for new cover letters, rewrites, or cover-letter-specific audits.

## Goal, reader, and inputs

Write a compact, persuasive English letter that explains **why verified applicant experiences equip the person to do this specific job**, not a prose duplication of the resume. Typical audiences are HR and hiring managers, not the research-paper reviewer. Request the actual JD for role-specific drafting; take current employer/team, exact title, Req ID, source of posting if known, channel, closing date, CV and verified Evidence Bank, original rubric, length, tone, recipient and formatting requirements.

## The complete drafting sequence

1. **Decompose the JD**: identify 3–5 central responsibilities/abilities, hard eligibility and preferred criteria, team constraints, and relevant organizational context. Do not write empty generic brand praise.
2. **Evidence matching**: select only 1–3 highly relevant, supportable examples. For each, connect role requirement → factual experience → personal action and output → value to the employer. Do not force relevance where it does not exist.
3. **Narrative first**: decide a one-sentence applicant value proposition before drafting. Avoid "passionate, hardworking, fast learner" without example evidence.
4. **Structure**: default to **200–250 English words**, ordinarily **no more than 300**, unless the target explicitly requires another length. Choose **three or four paragraphs** based on information density, not a fixed template:
   - Opening: exact role and ID (where known), reliable job-posting source (if known), authentic reason for applying, accurate school/degree/major where relevant, and one true headline strength (usually 2–3 sentences).
   - Technical/analytical evidence: one or two strong examples, concrete applicant actions, outcomes, and why they matter to this job. Explain specialist work accessibly.
   - Distinct soft-skill/leadership evidence **only if useful**: specific coordination, problem solving, communication, initiative or learning examples the JD actually values; avoid padding.
   - Motivation and close: precise team or role fit, contribution the applicant could make, interest in discussion/interview, a concise courteous close (usually 2–3 sentences).
   - Three-paragraph variant merges technical and soft-skill evidence into the middle section. All versions preserve opening → evidence → closing.
5. **Write for HR**: clear active verbs, natural first-person voice when appropriate, short sentences, necessary technical terms only. Avoid beginning every sentence with "I", but do not apply a blanket academic prohibition against "I"/"we". Tools and acronyms are not selling points without demonstrated results.
6. **Balance evidence**: a 70% soft / 30% hard split for entry-level applicants can serve as a rough example, never a mandatory ratio; the JD decides the correct balance.
7. **Truth boundary**: strengthen phrasing and select relevant stories without changing the facts. Never invent employer, published paper, independent ownership, shipped deployment, experiment, real users, numbers, awards, accomplishments or availability.
8. **Business-letter conventions**: use a clean name/contact header, date, confirmed recipient/team details, professional salutation, paragraphs, a "Sincerely" or appropriate close, and signature. Reserve signature space for print. Use "Enclosure: Resume" or "My resume is enclosed" **only when the resume is actually attached**. Do not make up the addressee name or postal address.
9. **Recruiter reverse scan**: can a reader immediately identify the role, evidence-backed matching strengths and credible reason to consider the applicant? Delete clichés, irrelevant history, résumé repetition and jargon without proof.

## Extra rules often lost in consolidation

- In an opening, prioritize correct role title/Req ID, actual job source if known, genuine interest, real institution/major/degree and one truthful matching strength. Do not cram all fields into one paragraph solely to follow a checklist.
- Use the official English spelling of organizations; e.g., Shanghai Jiao Tong University. Familiar abbreviations after the first full use are fine.
- State genuine availability, onsite/hybrid/remote arrangements or language abilities only when relevant and confirmed; never imply an unverified work commitment.
- A numerical achievement in an example template is **not** an applicant achievement; never copy dummy publication or employment examples.
- The letter should be usually readable on **one page**, using legible **10–12 pt** type, balanced whitespace and appropriate margins unless a different official template is prescribed.
- If applying **by email**, provide a clear subject featuring name + target position/identifier, where appropriate.
- An AI-use disclosure belongs in the application **only when officially requested**. If required, accurately describe the actual model, its role and any necessary prompt/response appendix. Never append default disclosure to an HR letter.

## Evaluation and revision

Use the user's actual rubric when present; otherwise inspect six qualitative dimensions: **Clarity, Conciseness, Design, Audience, Accuracy, Content**. Screen hard failures (wrong role, fictitious/contradictory facts, page/word limit, spelling/grammar) before soft qualities. Evidence-ground each critique; assign numerical scores only with a defined scoring scale. Revise and perform a **fresh independent read**, retaining missing facts for applicant verification.

## Deliverable

For a complete writing assignment normally provide:
- **Writing strategy**: at most five concise points about JD priorities and story choice.
- **Final cover letter**: the English document, ready to copy after factual checks.
- **Rubric/review**: detailed only when review/end-to-end mode is requested, plus corrections and open verification items outside the letter.

If the user wants only wording or a short draft, return only that without adding paperwork.
